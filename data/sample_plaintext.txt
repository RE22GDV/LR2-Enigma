THE SIGNAL SECTION REPORTS THAT THE ENEMY FLEET HAS LEFT THE SOUTHERN ANCHORAGE
AND IS MOVING SLOWLY ALONG THE COAST UNDER COVER OF HEAVY WEATHER. OUR SCOUTS
COUNTED ELEVEN TRANSPORTS AND AT LEAST FOUR ESCORT VESSELS, BUT THE VISIBILITY
WAS POOR AND THE NUMBER MAY BE HIGHER THAN THAT. THE HARBOUR COMMANDER ASKS FOR
IMMEDIATE INSTRUCTIONS BECAUSE THE DEFENSIVE BATTERIES CANNOT COVER BOTH
APPROACHES AT THE SAME TIME, AND THE SUPPLY CONVOY IS NOT EXPECTED BEFORE THE
END OF THE WEEK. IF THE WEATHER CLEARS DURING THE NIGHT WE SHOULD BE ABLE TO
OBSERVE THE COLUMN FROM THE NORTHERN RIDGE AND SEND A CORRECTED ESTIMATE AT
FIRST LIGHT. UNTIL THEN EVERY AVAILABLE MAN MUST REMAIN AT HIS POST AND THE
RESERVE COMPANY SHOULD BE HELD READY TO MOVE WITHOUT FURTHER ORDERS.

THE ENGINEERS HAVE FINISHED THE SECOND LINE OF TRENCHES ABOVE THE OLD MILL AND
HAVE BEGUN TO LAY WIRE ACROSS THE OPEN GROUND BETWEEN THE ROAD AND THE RIVER.
PROGRESS IS SLOWER THAN PLANNED BECAUSE THE GROUND IS FROZEN AND THE PROMISED
MATERIAL ARRIVED ONLY IN PART. THE FIELD HOSPITAL HAS MOVED INTO THE SCHOOL
BUILDING NEAR THE SQUARE, WHERE THERE IS CLEAN WATER AND ENOUGH ROOM FOR ABOUT
SIXTY BEDS. THE DOCTOR REQUESTS A FURTHER DELIVERY OF BANDAGES AND FUEL FOR THE
GENERATOR, AND WARNS THAT WITHOUT THEM HE CANNOT KEEP THE OPERATING ROOM IN USE
FOR MORE THAN A FEW DAYS.

COMMUNICATION WITH THE FORWARD OBSERVATION POST WAS INTERRUPTED FOR SEVERAL
HOURS THIS MORNING WHEN A SHELL CUT THE TELEPHONE CABLE IN THREE PLACES. THE
LINE HAS SINCE BEEN REPAIRED, BUT WE SHOULD ASSUME THAT IT WILL FAIL AGAIN AND
PREPARE A SECOND ROUTE THROUGH THE WOOD. THE RADIO SET REMAINS OUR ONLY
RELIABLE MEANS OF CONTACT WITH HEADQUARTERS, AND FOR THAT REASON EVERY MESSAGE
SENT OVER IT MUST BE ENCIPHERED WITHOUT EXCEPTION, EVEN WHEN THE CONTENT SEEMS
ENTIRELY ROUTINE. THE OPERATORS HAVE BEEN REMINDED THAT REPEATED PHRASES AND
FIXED OPENINGS MAKE THE WORK OF AN ENEMY ANALYST VERY MUCH EASIER, AND THAT THE
SAME KEY SHOULD NEVER BE USED FOR TWO DIFFERENT DISPATCHES ON THE SAME DAY.

WE EXPECT THE RELIEF BATTALION TO REACH THE VILLAGE SOME TIME AFTER MIDNIGHT.
THEIR GUIDES KNOW THE PATH ALONG THE STREAM AND SHOULD AVOID THE MAIN ROAD,
WHICH IS UNDER OBSERVATION FROM THE HILLS. ONCE THEY ARRIVE, THE TIRED COMPANIES
WILL WITHDRAW IN GOOD ORDER, TAKING WITH THEM THE WOUNDED AND WHATEVER STORES
CAN BE CARRIED. NOTHING OF VALUE IS TO BE LEFT BEHIND, AND THE BRIDGE BELOW THE
MILL IS TO BE DESTROYED AS SOON AS THE LAST SECTION HAS CROSSED IT.
